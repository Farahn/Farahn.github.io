The Equity Equation Project

Role
Principal investigator
Funding
UK FCDO, through DARE-RC
Partners
CITY@LUMS and PAMS
Team
Basit Rahim, Ghamae Jamal and Anees Amjad

This project brings existing data sources together in a GIS framework and uses agent-based simulation to test how policy choices could change school enrolment for different groups of children in Lahore. The aim is to show planners which barriers keep children out of school and which interventions would reach them. Results presented in 2025 point to economic barriers as a major constraint on access.

Responsible AI Integration in Education in Pakistan: A Scoping Study

Role
Principal investigator
Funding
UK FCDO, through DARE-RC
Team
Dr Maryam Mustafa, Annum Sadiq, Usama Hameed and Mina Shoaib
Partners
Edkasa and SAHE

The study maps current and emerging uses of AI in Pakistani education across Punjab, Sindh and the Islamabad Capital Territory. It benchmarks national policy against international frameworks and gathers evidence from teachers, EdTech firms and civil society on equity, governance and safe implementation. The study held a National Convening on Responsible AI in Education at the Pakistan Institute of Education, Islamabad, in August 2026.

Awarded 2026

Beyond Western Defaults: A Socio-Technical Infrastructure for Community-Governed AI Alignment in Low-Resource Contexts

Role
Co-principal investigator
Principal investigator
Dr Maryam Mustafa
Funding
Google Society-Centered AI Research Award

Large language models already give health, education and legal guidance in Pakistan, yet the benchmarks that decide whether they work reflect Western linguistic and cultural defaults. This project builds infrastructure through which communities in low-resource settings can govern how AI systems are aligned and evaluated. It is grounded in two deployments: Awaaz-e-Sehat, a voice-enabled maternal health platform, and a speech-first literacy and numeracy app for out-of-school adolescent girls in Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

Political Economy Analysis of Assessment Data Use in Pakistan

Role
Principal investigator
Funding
UK FCDO, through DARE-RC
Team
Areej Mahmood, Basit Rahim and Muhammad Shehbaz
Partner
SAHE

This study examines how political and institutional incentives shape the production, interpretation and use of assessment data across federal, provincial and district levels of Pakistan's education system. It combines predictive modelling with a multi-tier political economy analysis to identify where assessment data informs decisions and where it does not.

Digital Evolution in Teacher Development: Analysis of Punjab's Continuous Professional Development (CPD) System

Role
Research lead
Principal investigator
Nighat Lone, DARE-RC
Team
Nimra Tariq, Zariya Mushtaq, Yassal Arif and Dr Jessica Albrent
Funding
UK FCDO, through DARE-RC

This mixed-methods study examines how Punjab's digitised continuous professional development system, built around the Classroom Observation Tool, shapes teaching in public primary schools. It combines just over 135,000 digitised classroom observations from 2021 to 2024 with interviews and classroom visits in Lahore, Rawalpindi, Multan and Muzaffargarh involving teachers, head teachers, Assistant Education Officers and district and provincial officials. Data travels upward quickly, but support rarely travels back to teachers, and lessons staged for observation visits suggest that high scores can reflect performance for monitoring rather than better teaching.

  • Report Final report, 2025
  • Policy brief Digital evolution in teacher development, 2026
  • Blog The digital doorway, 2026
  • Paper From data to decisions, AERA 2025
  • Talk Policy dialogue on teacher effectiveness, 2025

Comparative Analysis of Continuous Professional Development (CPD) Models Across Pakistan: Design, Delivery, and Perceptions

Role
Co-principal investigator
Principal investigators
Zain Murtaza Maken, Areej Mahmood and Shumile Fatima
Funding
UK FCDO, through DARE-RC
Implementing organisation
PAMS

This scoping study compares the continuous professional development models of all four provinces and the Federal capital: how each is designed, governed and delivered, how it uses technology, who takes part, and how teachers judge its value for the classroom. It combines a structured review of policy documents and research with interviews and focus groups with federal and provincial officials, CPD institutions, district managers, development partners and teachers, analysed through the RISE Education Systems Diagnostic and the What Works Hub for Global Education Implementation Framework. The outputs are comparable CPD profiles and province-specific policy options for stronger teacher development.

Pilot, 2026

Automated Assessment of Urdu Oral Reading Fluency

Focus
Children aged 7 to 10 reading Urdu
Method
Speech recognition benchmarked against expert scoring
Team
Maheen Salman

This pilot benchmarks multilingual speech recognition models on recordings of children reading Urdu passages, comparing automated words-correct-per-minute scores with expert human scoring. Errors are analysed by age, gender, school location and passage difficulty. The results will inform a costed plan for an offline, teacher-facing tool for formative reading assessment.

2016 to 2020

Language Use in K-16 STEM Education (Doctoral Research)

Institution
University of Washington
Advisor
Professor Mari Ostendorf
Funding
US$500K NSF grant that I helped write

My dissertation developed natural language processing tools to analyse language in K-16 STEM materials and validated them against student performance. It estimated the linguistic complexity of science texts and assessment items, measured the representation and agency of feminine and masculine characters in K-12 texts, and showed that item features such as linguistic complexity have non-linear effects on item difficulty. The work rested on a formal partnership between Electrical and Computer Engineering and the College of Education.

  • Thesis Automatic analysis of language use in K-16 STEM education, 2020
  • Paper Estimating linguistic complexity for science texts, BEA 2018
  • Paper Language based mapping of science assessment items to skills, BEA 2017
  • Code Linguistic complexity models
  • Code BCA text classifier

2018 to 2019

Automated Essay Scoring with Discourse-Aware Models

Setting
Research internship, Liulishuo, San Mateo, California

This work built neural essay scoring models for English language learners that account for text coherence, reducing the need for hand-crafted features and human scoring. The goal was writing feedback that resource-constrained classrooms could use at scale.

About

Dr Farah Nadeem is an Assistant Professor at the Syed Ahsan Ali and Syed Maratib Ali School of Education at the Lahore University of Management Sciences (LUMS). Her research brings machine learning and education data to questions of assessment, teaching quality and fair access to school.

She leads studies funded by the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office on the political economy of assessment data, responsible AI in education and the simulation of school enrolment, and is co-principal investigator on a Google Society-Centered AI Research Award. Earlier, she led monitoring and evaluation for Punjab's school education reform programme, where her team ran data collection for 52,000 schools, and she has worked with UNICEF Pakistan and the World Bank's Education Global Practice. From 2023 to 2025 she directed the LUMS Office of Accessibility and Inclusion.

She holds a PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering from the University of Washington, where she was a Fulbright Fellow.
